How to Play

A playable Tamil Nadu rule set
1

Objective

Move all four of your pieces from your home base, around the shared track, up your private home path, and into the centre before the AI does.

2

The Board

A cross-shaped track shared by both sides, with a private final stretch for each player leading to the centre.

3

The Dayam

Two brass Dayam pieces are thrown together. Each lands showing either its marked or plain face. The combination decides your move.

4

Starting a Piece

A piece leaves your home base only on a special throw (4 or 6). It enters directly onto your start cell.

5

Movement

Move any piece already on the board forward by the exact number shown. You must be able to land exactly — overshooting the final cell is not allowed.

6

Capturing

Land exactly on an opponent's piece on an unprotected cell to send it back to their home base.

7

Safe Cells

Cells marked with a small engraved diamond are protected — no capturing takes place there.

8

Home Path

After a full circuit of the shared track, a piece turns into your own coloured path, private to you, leading to the centre.

9

Extra Turns

Throwing a 4 or a 6 is a special throw — it earns you another roll immediately, as long as you had a legal move to make.

10

Winning

The first side to bring all four pieces home wins.

Dayakattam has regional variations across Tamil Nadu. This digital version follows one documented playable rule set, and the rules engine is kept separate from the presentation so regional variants can be added later.

About Dayakattam

Cultural context

Dayakattam — also known in places as Thaayam — belongs to a wider family of traditional South Indian race-and-strategy games, related to Pachisi, Chaupar and Pallanguzhi-adjacent throwing games played across the subcontinent.

Rules varied by region, family and community, passed on by demonstration rather than by a fixed rulebook. Equipment traditionally included brass or wooden throwing sticks, and cowrie shells, depending on local tradition. This project does not claim a single precise origin date — the exact age and lineage of these games is not something we can state reliably here.

This digital version implements one defined, playable ruleset, built so that other regional variants can be layered onto the same engine in future.

Why it matters

Games are often preserved through people, not books. Traditional games can quietly disappear when generations stop teaching them, when play moves indoors and digital, when local practices become less common, and when regional variations are simply forgotten. This project exists to keep one playable form of Dayakattam accessible — not to replace the version your own family may play.

Fair Play

How the dice really work

Every Dayam throw is generated independently using your browser's cryptographic random source before any animation begins. The tumbling animation you see is then resolved to match that already-decided result — the animation never determines the outcome, and outcomes are never scripted or predetermined.

The AI opponent uses the exact same dice mechanism and the exact same legal-move rules as you. It cannot see future rolls, and it cannot bend the rules in its own favour. Any advantage it has comes purely from stronger decision-making.
